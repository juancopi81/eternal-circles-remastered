# Ideas

Rough ideas for Eternal Circles, kept here until there is time to explore them in a free session. Each
one is explored first as a throwaway prototype on its own branch; nothing reaches the live piece unless
Juan decides it has earned a place. Decisions that get made go to [DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md).

For each idea: who it came from and when, the idea in its owner's words, the questions to answer, and a
status (**inbox**, **exploring**, **adopted** or **dropped**). Two questions apply to all of them: does
it make the piece more itself, and can it stay in the browser (F13)?

## Ripples that crash
- **From:** Juan, 2026-11-03, with a screenshot.
- **Idea:** where the ripples from two discs meet, they crash, and the crash makes a new sound.
- **Questions:**
  - What does a crash sound like: a soft splash, a bell both voices share, or the interval between
    their two notes?
  - Only the first touch of two ripple fronts, or every crossing? Bells ripple on every note, so
    crashes could pile up fast.
  - Should the crash sound where the ripples meet, panned left or right with it?
- **Status:** inbox

## The piece draws as it plays
- **From:** Juan, 2026-11-03.
- **Idea:** the music leaves an image behind. Events leave points (perhaps where ripples crash, perhaps
  something else), some points connect into lines, the lines make a form, and the form is shown
  briefly in 3D.
- **Questions:**
  - Which events leave points: crashes, notes, bells, or each melody's place in MusicVAE's latent
    space?
  - Which points connect: in the order they happened, the nearest ones, or the ones from the same
    voices?
  - What is the third dimension? Time is a natural one: a looping disc traced over time becomes a
    spiral, which would tie it to Brouwer's *La Espiral Eterna*.
  - When does it show: always, faintly on the floor, or when the piece goes idle?
  - Could the drawing be a keepsake, an image of your session to save or share? Juan's note on L6
    leaves the preview card open, so a session's drawing could become it.
- **Status:** inbox

## Möbius discs
- **From:** Juan, 2026-11-04, from his note on L2 in DECISIONS.md.
- **Idea:** a disc that works like a Möbius strip. It goes back to the 2020 inspiration: Jos Leys's
  video "J.S. Bach - Crab Canon on a Möbius Strip", about the Crab Canon from Bach's *Musical
  Offering*, the collection *Gödel, Escher, Bach* talks about in its first chapter. It is also why
  retrograde exists (F6).
- **What the research found (2026-11-04):**
  - In the Crab Canon the second voice plays the first one backwards. The mathematician Tony Phillips
    points out that the video's strip carries the score on both of its sides, which makes it a
    cylinder, not a Möbius strip; any repeating score can be drawn that way. The WTF Bach podcast makes
    the same case in its 2023 episode "The Crab Canon Isn't a Möbius Strip", the source of the second
    video, and argues that earlier analyses missed details of how the inversions work.
  - A score fits a true Möbius strip only if, halfway through its cycle, it comes back upside down (a
    glide reflection). That happens in a canon in contrary motion whose second voice enters exactly
    halfway.
  - Bach's examples are Canons 3 and 5 of the Fourteen Canons on the Goldberg ground (BWV 1087), found
    in 1974 in Bach's own copy of the Goldberg Variations. In Canon 3 the second voice plays the first
    eight notes of the Goldberg bass upside down. Other contrary-motion canons, such as Canons 3 and 9
    of the *Musical Offering*, don't fit.
  - So our discs already have the Crab Canon's true shape: a loop, which is a cylinder, read backwards
    by retrograde (F6). A Möbius disc would add the move the piece doesn't have yet: inversion.
- **Questions:**
  - One disc or two? One disc could play the melody on one turn and its inversion on the next. Two
    discs could form a real Möbius canon, as in BWV 1087: the second plays the melody upside down,
    entering exactly halfway, one bar in.
  - Which inversion: by scale steps, which stays in the key, or by semitones, which can leave it?
  - How does it look: the coin flip (M6) on every turn, or the disc drawn as a twisted band?
- **Sources:**
  - Tony Phillips, "Surface topology in Bach canons, I: The Möbius strip", AMS Feature Column, October
    2016: https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/~tony/whatsnew/oct16/canons/canons.html
  - WTF Bach, episode 54, "The Crab Canon Isn't a Möbius Strip" (2023):
    https://wtfbach.substack.com/p/the-crab-canon-isnt-a-mobius-strip-459; its video:
    https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0WX_gokLiJA
  - Jos Leys, "J.S. Bach - Crab Canon on a Möbius Strip": https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xUHQ2ybTejU
- **Status:** inbox
